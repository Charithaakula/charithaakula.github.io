Our synthesis

The perspectives concern policy capability, execution timing and orchestration. New papers add more concrete choices inside those categories: ImageWAM and LaWAM change the intermediate used for action prediction, while Rolling-WAM distributes joint denoising across successive updates.

Our reading is to compare complete execution loops, with their observation interfaces, startup costs, steady-state timing and recovery behavior visible. A compact predictive policy is not automatically a replacement for a supervisor or fast controller. These studies support scoped comparisons rather than a universally best architecture.

Perspective 03

Predictive optimization

SuperMimic · MoRE

Use predictive models in optimization, while distinguishing training-time policy updates from control-time action refinement.

Talks
SuperMimicMoRE
Papers & technical sources
  • MoRE: Mixture-of-Experts for Multi-Physics Robotic World Models ↗Paper · linked
  • SuperMimic: Scaling Humanoid Whole-Body Control with One Differentiable World Model ↗Paper · linked
  • LeWAM: End-to-End World and Action Modeling with JEPAs ↗Primary paper reviewed

Our synthesis

The new papers distinguish what prediction contributes: editing caches in ImageWAM, latent subgoals in LaWAM, rolling video-action context in Rolling-WAM, and explicit action-plan refinement in LeWAM. They do not measure the same use of a world model.

Our synthesis is to separate training-time gains from decision-time costs. LeWAM’s planning updates candidate actions while keeping model parameters frozen; its physical-robot evaluations use direct execution under a different protocol. Match goal information, task conditions and runtime budgets before treating results as evidence for adding online planning.

Our synthesis

The papers broaden representation beyond a coordinate choice. H2R-Bridge separates observation alignment from action decoding; GLAM grounds latent actions with target-robot supervision. H2RBench holds robot supervision fixed and finds that more human data has task- and method-dependent value.

Our reading is to account for coverage, interfaces and evaluation separately. Scale Up Strategically adds another question: which demonstrations should receive a fixed collection budget? Transfer evidence should retain its robot-data budget, environment assumptions and scoring definition; a weighted task-progress score should not become a binary success percentage.

Deployment corrections

FlowDAgger · Roberto Martín-Martín

Use intervention data to target behavior changes, then check performance on both old and new tasks.

  • Multisensory Continual Learning: Adapting Pretrained Visuomotor Policies to Force ↗Related context · Primary paper reviewed
  • FAR: Failure-Aware Retry for Test-Time Recovery and Continual Policy Improvement ↗Related context · Primary paper reviewed
  • ASPIRE: Agentic /Skills Discovery for Robotics ↗Related context · Primary paper reviewed

Our synthesis

The papers show different ways to use an existing model. RARA preserves representations using relevant retrieved trajectories; Memory Anchors selects replay states for retaining earlier tasks; MuSe adds force-torque observations while replaying original data. Those mechanisms spend additional data and compute in different places.

Our synthesis is to identify the actual bottleneck and account for the whole update budget. RARA’s extra alignment stage and access to relevant pretraining data matter; selective replay still needs diversity. These results motivate targeted adaptation and retention tests, rather than a single ranking of pretraining, post-training and corrections.

Our synthesis

The papers separate detecting a problem, retrying a task and improving a policy. ContactGuard predicts imminent-contact failures without supplying recovery; FAR studies retries and later policy updates, but its real tests use human outcome labels. ATOM-Bench examines held-out compositions under a controlled physical protocol.

Our reading is to report interventions, reset requirements, attempt budgets and untested settings alongside success. RLE-Bench measures bounded engineering workflows, not fielded generalist reliability. These distinctions sharpen the factory-versus-generality discussion without implying that one successful workflow establishes unrestricted transfer.

Our synthesis

The manuscripts distinguish the artifact that changes. PhysCaP updates physical beliefs and control programs without neural training; ASPIRE retains code and repair guidance; HARBOR and ENPIRE organize numerical policy-improvement experiments. EmbodiedSWE supplies verified simulation demonstrations for VLA fine-tuning.

Our synthesis is to identify the agent’s role and the human or engineered support around it. ASPIRE still needs real-world guidance and debugging; ENPIRE assumes prepared reset and verification interfaces; HARBOR’s gates do not guarantee semantic correctness. MobiAgent’s finite policy-update rounds plateau. These are useful improvements with stated limits, not evidence of unlimited recursive improvement.
